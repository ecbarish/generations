## The short version

**Decided (Evan, 2026-10-11): the Paint Shed comes first** of the new places. The Little Wagon and the Snack Kitchen
follow; the ranch's own steps (LR3 onward) continue alongside.

1. **One place, not more icons.** A toddler can't find her way around the arcade, but she already knows Little Ranch.
   So the new toddler games become **places inside Little Ranch**: from the ranch she taps a picture (a wagon, a
   kitchen door, a paint pot) and goes there with the same baby friends. One icon for her to find; every toy shares
   the creatures, sounds, grown-up lock and bedtime.
2. **Little Ranch's next steps** (each one small PR, in this order): the singing flowers, a second baby friend, rain
   and rainbows, hats to try on, and a longer bedtime routine.
3. **New toddler toys, best first:** **the Little Wagon** (load the babies onto a wagon and ride through the valley,
   matching colours at each stop), **the Snack Kitchen** (make a snack and watch who loves what), and **the Paint
   Shed** (big brushes, a creature to colour in). A shape puzzle waits.
4. **The rules stay:** no reading, nothing to fail, no timers, no links out or purchases, the grown-up lock, nothing
   stored but the sound choice, and every step tried with a real toddler before it is called done.

## Why the "one place" choice

The arcade homepage is built for grown-ups: many games, words on cards, links out. Little Ranch was made safe on
purpose (no links out, a grown-up lock), and a three-year-old learns one door and goes back to it. A separate
"Little Wagon" on the homepage would send her through the grown-up arcade every time. Inside Little Ranch, a new toy
is one tap from the baby she already loves, and the ranch's bedtime still ends the visit.

How it looks: once the baby has played a little, a picture-sign appears at the edge of the ranch (a wagon wheel, a
steaming pot, a paint drip). Tapping it walks the baby there; a big "home" picture (the barn) brings her back. No
words anywhere. Bedtime comes from wherever she is: the baby yawns and they all go home to sleep.

## Little Ranch: the next steps

Each step is one PR on `games/little-ranch/` with its checks in tests/little-ranch.html, a README changelog line, and a
short "tried with a toddler" note in the PR (Evan or another parent). Steps are small on purpose: a toddler notices
every new thing, and one at a time keeps it calm.

| Step | What she does | Why it's good for her age |
|---|---|---|
| **LR3 The singing flowers** (already the open next step) | A row of big flowers in the meadow; tap one and it sings its own note and bobs; the baby dances to whatever she plays | Music and cause and effect are the strongest toddler play; it was promised as a "room" in the first pitch |
| **LR4 A second friend** | A baby Pebblit (or a Glowmote) wanders in some visits; both can be fed and bubbled; they nuzzle each other | New friends keep a toy fresh without new rules; first step toward "Little Ranch with a few baby creatures" (GOALS.md) |
| **LR5 Rain and rainbows** | Tap the cloud: soft rain, puddles to splash with a tap; tap the sun: a rainbow, the baby looks up | Pure cause and effect, and a weather word to say together |
| **LR6 The hat basket** | Tap the basket; the baby tries on a hat (straw, flower crown, a tiny crown); tap again for the next | Dress-up and pretend play blossom around age 3; giggles guaranteed |
| **LR7 The bedtime routine** | Before sleep: a brush of teeth, a drink of water, "goodnight" to each friend with a wave, then the lullaby | Routines comfort toddlers; it mirrors her own bedtime, which parents value |
| **LR8 Spoken words** | Names, colours and "night night" spoken gently (the browser's voice first; recorded voices later) | Naming things together is how language grows at this age |

**An idea for later, Evan's call:** a grown-up could record their own voice for "night night" and her baby's name,
kept only on that device. Lovely, but it asks the browser for the microphone, so it needs a yes first.

### The Little Wagon: 17/18
A cosy wagon pulled by a gentle ranch creature. She taps each baby to hop aboard (they wave), then taps the wagon to
roll. It trundles through the valley past things to tap (a windmill that spins, a pond that splashes, sheep-like
woolies that baa), and stops at coloured gates: the gate is red, so she taps the red wagon car, and the gate swings
open with a cheer. Any car she taps does something nice; the right colour just gets the bigger cheer, so nothing is
ever wrong. Vehicles and riding are among the most-loved toddler themes, and colour matching is right at her age.
*Two birds:* the valley it rolls through is Wildbond's own, drawn small. *Smallest test:* load two babies, one ride
with three things to tap and one colour gate.

### The Snack Kitchen: 17/18
A little kitchen at the barn. She drags food (berries, a carrot, a bread roll, a jam jar) into a big bowl, taps the
whisk to stir, and serves it. Each baby has favourites and pulls a funny face at the rest (no wrong answers, just
reactions). Pretend cooking for someone else is classic play at 2 to 4, and *Toca Kitchen* is one of the best-loved
toddler apps for exactly this. *Smallest test:* four foods, one bowl, one baby who loves berries.

### The Paint Shed: 16/18
A big outline of her baby creature and four fat paint pots. Tap a pot, then tap anywhere on the creature: that part
fills with colour (no fiddly edges); a spray of sparkles when every part is coloured; the painted baby then walks out
in its new colours until bedtime. *Smallest test:* one outline, four colours, the walk-out.

### Waits: a shape puzzle (14/18)
Three or four big pieces of a creature picture that snap into place when dragged near. Good, but drag accuracy and
"the piece won't go there" frustration suit 3½ to 4 better; revisit as she grows.

## What a toddler needs (unchanged, and for every new place)

The rules in [games-for-everyone.md](games-for-everyone.md) still hold: no reading, taps and short drags only, huge
targets away from the edges, every touch does something, no scores, timers, lives or wrong answers, no links,
purchases, ads or data, the grown-up lock, about 5 to 10 minutes with a gentle ending, tablet and phone first and
offline once loaded. Research behind them: the American Academy of Pediatrics' advice for ages 2 to 5 (a little
high-quality media, ideally used together), and calm, open-ended toy apps such as *Pok Pok* (widely praised for its calm,
rule-free design), Sago Mini and Toca Boca.
