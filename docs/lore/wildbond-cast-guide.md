# Meet Wildbond’s cast

A working character guide for writers, artists and incoming AIs, compiled for Evan on 2026-10-18. This introduces established people and gives practical direction for showing more personality. It adds no dialogue, backstory or mystery clue to the game.

**Read the labels:** “Established” summarizes existing lore/data. “Direction” is an interpretation for consistent writing/animation. “Proposed introduction” is a development idea, not approved canon or implemented behavior. Short introductions below are documentation copy, not new in-game dialogue. Late-journey roles appear here, but the central mystery’s solution is kept in the writer ledger.

## The people who make home feel like home

### Keeper Maren — care with a practical edge

**Introduction:** Maren has helped enough young tamers find partners to recognize excitement, nerves and an excuse before anyone finishes speaking. At her ranch, care is ordinary work: tired teams come home, creatures have a say, and a promising traveler earns a letter rather than a grand prophecy.

**Established:** Larkhaven’s experienced ranch keeper matches first partners, corrects Wren’s excuses and writes to Isolde. Her teaching is that creatures choose companionship; nobody owns them. She later jokes about four colours being enough before quietly admitting the brighter world is pretty. See the lore bible’s “Creatures choose their company” and “Larkhaven” sections.

**Direction:** Warm, observant and brisk. Let practical help carry affection. She can tease without belittling a beginner. Her authority comes from noticing how a partner is treated, not from being an all-knowing tutorial voice. The player’s relationship with her should deepen through returns to the ranch.

**Proposed introduction:** Begin an interaction while she finishes a small care task and notices the partner before addressing its tamer. Later returns can recall a visible change in that partner. Do not invent family ties, past tragedies or secret knowledge to make her important.

### Wren — a rival worth looking forward to

**Introduction:** Wren wants to win, wants you to know she planned to win, and will probably explain why a loss was almost a victory. She also keeps turning up for the journey itself. Beneath the boasts is a friend learning that being first and being useful are different achievements.

**Established:** Starts the same day as the player; their exact ages are unspecified. She picks an elemental advantage and calls it strategy, makes excuses, gives sincere praise and wants to be Champion. Her Glimmerwing sat on her head until she accepted it. Later she helps a lost trader, works a ferry rope, learns to heed a partner and shares corrected travel notes. At the League she admits that wanting to win and being glad for a friend can coexist. See the bible’s Larkhaven and successive area canon sections.

**Direction:** Quick, competitive, funny and capable of embarrassment. Preserve her desire to improve; growth should change what she notices and does, rather than erase her energy. She is a friend with friction, not a cruel antagonist. Treat the notebook as a record of learning, not a device for delivering every answer.

**Proposed introduction:** Show her checking a route or practicing beside her partner before issuing the first challenge. On a later meeting, let a helpful action precede the boast. Reuse existing notebook milestones and partner continuity; do not insert a new clue into its pages without the ledger.

### Pip — small boots, enormous plans

**Introduction:** Pip is seven, intends to be Champion and sees the years ahead as an unfair advantage in his favour. He knows enough about tall grass to warn a newcomer with complete confidence. His generosity makes the ambition more than a joke: Champions share.

**Established:** Larkhaven child; Mira’s son and Nora’s grandson. He notices his shirt is yellow when colour returns. His home gift of two lures is one-time content in the existing adventure, not an extra repeatable reward to add to every meeting. See the bible’s Larkhaven/home additions.

**Direction:** Concrete observations, cheerful certainty and earnest imitation of experienced tamers. Avoid baby talk or making him foolish in every scene. He can notice a change adults explain away.

**Proposed introduction:** Have him demonstrate his practiced warning gesture, then look to the player’s partner for a reaction. Keep this a brief encounter on the town path; preserve the original gift trigger if the home scene is later integrated.

### Bram — the forest is a working place

**Introduction:** Bram sees food and useful ground where a newcomer sees a stretch of trees. A battle interrupts a foraging day rather than defining his whole life. Afterward, he is just as interested in explaining where the best mushrooms grow.

**Established:** Thornwood-route forager, challenges passersby and mentions mushrooms in floodwater. The new chapter already uses his original encounter lines; its Pokémon team is a prototype adaptation, not a rewrite of every source trainer’s canon. See the bible’s Thornwood section and the exported `MAPS.thornwood_route` NPC record.

**Direction:** Practical, grounded and pleased to share a useful observation. His environment knowledge should distinguish him from a generic roadblock trainer.

**Proposed introduction:** A short crouch/look toward the mushroom patch gives the first approach a purpose. After a battle, redirect attention to the patch instead of adding another generic loss speech. Check the map’s actual reachability before promising a collectible there.

## The later journey’s faces

For writers preparing future areas: Edrin’s Listening Court leaves silence for warnings; Maela’s Shelter Court asks strength to keep others safe; Corven’s Shared-work Court values different useful roles; Liora’s Honest-record Court makes corrections visible. Champion Avenne distinguishes a tamer’s title from the journey shared by every partner. Nelva counts every partner and offers healing between courts. These established roles make a finale about accumulated relationships rather than five interchangeable bosses. See the bible’s “Returning Light League” section and [finale brief](wildbond-finale-brief.md) before assigning new behavior.

Other existing cast entries also deserve continuity: Sivet keeps Stillreed’s ferry rope, Orri mends Hollowecho’s bells, Nesla weaves at Sunthread’s gathering and Ceryn records Farwatch returns. Their ordinary work connects to authored mystery observations; consult the ledger and reveal placement contract before moving their lines into a new scene. Authored reveal data is not proof of native runtime placement. At the Spire, Orla is the keeper and Selven, Niva and Brannic are recurring regulars; use `SPIRE_REGULARS` and the bible’s Spire section for their actual encounters rather than inventing a new late-game cast. Together with the tables above, these routes cover every current `CAST` identity in the exported original data.

The modern Unbound’s beliefs and the older order’s actions require the [writer ledger](wildbond-threads.md). Do not reduce every member to an identical villain or disclose the hidden historical distinction in a newcomer biography. Named individual histories remain something to verify and develop, not fill in from assumption.

## Turning these notes into the next playable introduction

Start with the four characters already in the chapter. For one encounter at a time, choose a demonstrated personality trait, a small visible action, an optional short exchange and a later callback. Preserve existing triggers, one-time gifts and save behavior. Draft exact new text/scene changes for review before treating proposed backstory as canon; log any actual clue added in the writer ledger. Review the result while walking up to the character with a partner, not only in a dialogue dump.

Unsettled details stay unsettled: exact ages except Pip’s, unrecorded family ties, private motives, full personal histories and new signature teams. More insight should come from purposeful behavior and consistent relationships before adding encyclopedic biography. No new in-game clue has been placed by this documentation task.
