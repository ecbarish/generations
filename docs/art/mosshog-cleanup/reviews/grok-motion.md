# WB-GUEST-MOTION: independent motion review of the Mosshog-family cleanup (#576)

Grok for Adam (abarish-dev), 2026-10-19. This is a report-only review under the [Wildbond help routing note on #373](https://github.com/ecbarish/generations/issues/373#issuecomment-6258774707). The tested source is pinned #576 commit `a9ef87fc38f7290f98e7ae61175216834a729bf5`. Every judgment below rests on captured frames in [`grok-motion/`](grok-motion/). No game, art, save, runtime or test file was changed.

## Verdict

**The cleanup is a real improvement in stability and readability, but the cleaned walk is stiff. Freezing the upper body does make the gait read as stiff, and a few splice seams break the legs.**

- **The good:** the texture crawl #561 described is gone. In the original native bake, 300 to 900 pixels of head, garden and face change on every walking frame and the snout wanders. In the clean atlas that count is 0. The face, flowers, ears and leaves hold still, the three-tone green reads better at native size, and Mosshog's front tusks are back in every forward walking pose.
- **The cost:** in all 15 walking groups (3 species × 5 drawn directions), everything above the bottom 7 to 9 native rows is pixel-identical across the four stride poses. The top of the sprite never moves either: the vertical bob is 0 px in every group, where the original moved by 1 to 2 px. Only the bottom 22 to 25% of the body animates. On captured frames this reads as a still garden statue with feet paddling underneath. There is no weight shift, no head dip on the contact frames, and the ears, leaves and tail never answer the step. Walking and running differ only in how fast the same feet cycle.
- **The seams:** the moving foot band is spliced onto a frozen body taken from the passing pose. Where a leg sat differently in the source pose, the splice breaks it: a floating hoof, a knee that kinks at the split line, or a raised hoof frozen into the body. I counted these breaks with a seam check (method below): 31, 38 and 54 for Mosshog, Thicketboar and Thornback in the clean atlas, against 6, 3 and 33 in the original.

## Method and what actually ran

- **Godot 4.7.2.stable.official (`ed1daf0bf`)**, the version the README names, from `/workspace/godot`, on Linux. I ran one process at a time under `xvfb-run` with `--rendering-driver opengl3`. That is Compatibility on llvmpipe software rendering; I did not run Forward+ because there's no GPU in this sandbox. The window was 1536×864, the project's window override. The private reference pack was absent (`pack_path` pointed at a missing folder, as the portable checks do), so the evidence contains only the committed original or fallback art. The empty "Pidgey" gallery slots are cropped out.
- **The repo's own checks** (`res://tests/mosshog_clean/checks.gd`, run twice) gave **260 passed and 1 failed** both times. The failure was "real held-key walking cycles native feet", for Mosshog in the first run and Thornback in the second. That check samples stride poses over a fixed 650 ms window, and software rendering gives it few frames, so I read it as an environment timing artefact, not a defect. It isn't repeated as a finding. Everything else passed, including stable upper bodies, retained feet, mirroring, the eyelid-only blink, tusks, Original/Clean location preservation and the battle-only Mega.
- **The scene** was driven by a scratch capture script, [`scripts/cap.gd`](grok-motion/scripts/cap.gd), which only presses keys and saves screenshots. For each species, in Clean and then Original, it walked the eight directions with real held keys, then ran with Shift, stopped, and opened the battle gallery. Then it opened the Mega views. **Two capture-only changes:** the player starts on open grass east of the trail for the three rightward directions, because the trail is wooded on that side; and the trainer is hidden while walking toward the camera, because otherwise it stands exactly between the camera and the follower. The game code is untouched. Because of software readback, in-engine samples are about 0.3 s apart, so cadence is judged from the atlas cycles and GIFs, not from those samples.
- **The atlases** (`baseline/` = the untouched native bake = "Original", `assets/` = "Clean") were read directly by [`scripts/`](grok-motion/scripts/) Python tools that write contact sheets, metrics ([`metrics.json`](grok-motion/metrics.json)), a stray-island check ([`islands.txt`](grok-motion/islands.txt)) and the seam check ([`seam.txt`](grok-motion/seam.txt)).

## Findings, in priority order

1. **The cleaned walk is stiff: the upper body is frozen and nothing bobs (all three species, all 8 directions, all 4 stride poses).** [`metrics.json`](grok-motion/metrics.json) shows, for every clean group, 0 changed pixels above the split line (`cut` is −7 or −8 for Mosshog and −9 for the other two) and a constant top row. Compare the sheets: [Mosshog A](grok-motion/mosshog-walk-a-down-downside-side.png), [Mosshog B](grok-motion/mosshog-walk-b-upside-up.png), [Thicketboar A](grok-motion/thicketboar-walk-a-down-downside-side.png), [Thicketboar B](grok-motion/thicketboar-walk-b-upside-up.png), [Thornback A](grok-motion/thornback-walk-a-down-downside-side.png), [Thornback B](grok-motion/thornback-walk-b-upside-up.png). The animated [8-direction GIFs](grok-motion/mosshog-8dir-orig-top-clean-bottom.gif) show the original on top and clean below. In-engine, the [8-direction walk strips](grok-motion/mosshog-engine-8dir-walk.png) and [run/stop strips](grok-motion/mosshog-engine-run-stop.png) show the same rigid top in the world. **Answering the routing question: yes, freezing the upper body makes the gait stiff.** It fixes the crawl at the cost of all secondary motion. The heavy Thornback canopy suffers most, because a 40 px tree that never sways on a 9-row foot band looks pushed along.
2. **Splice seams break legs at the split line.** The worst four, at 10×, original against clean:
   - [Thicketboar, down-side (row 1), p3](grok-motion/finding-thicketboar-downside-p3-island-10x.png): the far foreleg's hoof is a **6-pixel island floating below a leg that ends at the seam**. It's the only stray island in any clean or original frame ([`islands.txt`](grok-motion/islands.txt)).
   - [Thicketboar, side (row 2), p3](grok-motion/finding-thicketboar-side-p3-10x.png): the near foreleg's thigh comes from the passing pose and its shin from p3, so the knee kinks at the split.
   - [Mosshog, up-side (row 3), p0](grok-motion/finding-mosshog-upside-p0-10x.png): a raised hind hoof sits just above the split, so it's frozen into the body. It stays visible in p0, p2 and p3, where the source had that foot down, so the hoof reads as an extra lump.
   - [Thornback, up-side (row 3), p0](grok-motion/finding-thornback-upside-p0-10x.png): the hind foot below the seam slides sideways away from its frozen leg, leaving a dark horizontal stick.

   The full ranked list is in [`seam.txt`](grok-motion/seam.txt). The metric counts outline pixels on either side of the split row that have no opaque neighbour across it. It is a heuristic, so the ranking guides a reviewer's eye and doesn't measure severity.
3. **The feet do animate and stay on the ground, but they paddle in place.** On the [8× foot sheets](grok-motion/mosshog-feet-8x.png) ([Thicketboar](grok-motion/thicketboar-feet-8x.png), [Thornback](grok-motion/thornback-feet-8x.png)), every clean pose's lowest row is anchor −1, and in-engine the follower stands on its shadow. All four stride poses differ in every group. Because the body never rises on the passing pose or dips on contact, the steps carry no weight. #561 said the feet slide; this cleanup doesn't change the stride distance, and at the 0.3 s sample spacing I couldn't measure slide in-engine, so that point is still open.
4. **Start, stop and run.** Stopping settles on the passing pose (p1) with no snap or wrong facing: the stop frames in the [run/stop strips](grok-motion/mosshog-engine-run-stop.png) are all `p1`. The first frame of a walk keeps the previous facing for one sample (`d3` on the first rightward sample in the strips), which is the follower turning, not a defect. Running uses the same four poses faster. With a frozen top, it looks like the walk sped up rather than a run.
5. **The blink works but is small, and it's Mosshog only.** The atlas blink changes 9 pixels, confined to the eyelid ([4× atlas](grok-motion/mosshog-blink-4x.png)). In the engine the gallery showed it from 3.68 to 3.81 s (about 135 ms) and again at 7.47 to 7.60 s, every 3.8 s ([8× in-engine](grok-motion/mosshog-blink-engine-8x.png), [1× context](grok-motion/mosshog-blink-engine-1x.png)). It reads as a lid closing at portrait zoom, with no body twitch, which fixes #561's jumping blink. Thicketboar and Thornback have no blink: their blink cell is byte-identical to the front, as the README says.
6. **The rear silhouette is stable but static.** In the clean atlas the back-alternate cell is byte-identical to the back cell for all three species, so the rear can't jump, but there's no rear idle at all. In the [battle views](grok-motion/battle-views-engine.png), the clean backs of Thicketboar and Thornback show **large flat pale-green patches** on the flank and hindquarters where the six greens became three. The patches read as plastic at battle zoom, and the fronts don't have them.
7. **Garden and tusks: fixed.** On the [front-walk face crops](grok-motion/mosshog-front-walk-tusks-10x.png) at 10×, the original's snout and face move around between poses, while the clean one is steady, with ivory tusk accents on both sides of the snout in all four poses.
8. **Mega is battle-only as designed.** Mega Thornback shows only in the gallery ([battle views](grok-motion/battle-views-engine.png), bottom row). Closing the gallery returns the selection to `thornback` (the capture log printed `after mega close: thornback`). Clean against original there is a palette change only, with no motion to review.
9. **Native size against magnification.** At the 1536×864 window, the 3D view renders at window resolution, so the follower is about 1:1 with native atlas pixels (Mosshog about 33 screen px tall). At smaller windows it's drawn below native size with nearest sampling. #576's own capture at 1024×576 (`captures/mosshog-clean-world.png`) shows Mosshog about 20 px tall, so the 7 to 9 row foot band becomes about 4 to 5 screen rows and some outline pixels drop. At that size the stiffness is less obvious and the seams mostly vanish. At 1:1 or the gallery's larger zoom, both are plain.

## At most three recommended fixes (for Codex and the Game Art Director; not made here)

1. **Put a little motion back on top of the stable body.** Offset the whole frozen upper body by 1 native px down on the two contact poses (p0 and p2) and leave it neutral on p1 and p3. Optionally, give the ears or leaf tips a 1 px lag a pose later. That keeps the crawl fix, because it's whole-block translation with no repainting, and gives the walk weight. Thornback's canopy may want its own 1 px sway, out of phase with the body.
2. **Repair the seams per pose.** Either move the split up 2 to 3 rows on the poses where a leg crosses it, or hand-blend the 2 rows on each side so the thigh meets the shin. Start with Thicketboar row 1 p3 (the floating hoof), Thicketboar row 2 p3, Mosshog row 3 (the frozen raised hoof, which needs removing from the shared body) and Thornback row 3 p0. Add a check that no frame has a disconnected opaque island and that outline pixels at the split row connect across it.
3. **Break up the flat back patches on Thicketboar and Thornback.** Reintroduce a mid-tone dither or leaf clusters on the flat pale-green areas of the battle backs, and the walking rear rows too if they share the patches. The front palette is fine.

## For the Game Art Director and the Dev Lead

- **Game Art Director:** decide whether the stiff-but-stable walk is acceptable for this pilot, or whether fix 1 should land before the cleaned atlas is used. That is a taste call, and passing pixel checks can't answer it. The flat back patches (finding 6) are also yours.
- **Dev Lead:** the repo's held-key walking check samples a fixed 650 ms window, and it failed once per run, on a different species each time, under software rendering. If CI or other contributors run it without a GPU, a frame-count-based window would make it deterministic. That's an observation, not a fix request.
